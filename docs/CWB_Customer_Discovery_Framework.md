### Interview flow for 60 minutes

| Time | Purpose | Lead question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Set context | Which operational or visitor outcomes matter most, and why now? |
| 5–15 min | Map the work | Walk me through a typical day, from opening to closing. Who does what? |
| 15–35 min | Explore 2–3 scenarios | Tell me about the last time this happened. What happened next? |
| 35–45 min | Explore exceptions and constraints | When does this process become difficult or fail? How do people recover? |
| 45–55 min | Prioritize outcomes | Which problems have the greatest impact? What evidence shows improvement? |
| 55–60 min | Confirm and close | What did I misunderstand? Who else should I observe or speak with? |

## Operational and quality constraints

Ask what conditions a solution must work under. Capture the consequence of failing each constraint and who can confirm it. Convert terms such as “fast”, “secure”, or “easy” into measurable criteria later; do not invent thresholds during the interview.

| Area | Questions to ask | Target or constraint and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roles and access | Who may view or change records? Who approves overrides? Are shared devices used? |  |
| Privacy and data | What personal or location data is necessary, if any? Who needs access, for how long, and why? |  |
| Usability and accessibility | Which staff, volunteers, or visitors use it? What training, language, access, or assistance needs exist? |  |
| Performance and capacity | Which actions are time critical? How many people, boats, or concurrent activities occur at peak times? |  |
| Availability and connectivity | When must it work? Where is connectivity unreliable? What must continue during an outage? |  |
| Accuracy and freshness | How current must boat status or other operational data be? What happens when records conflict? |  |
| Recovery and audit | What can be lost, and for how long? Who resolves failed updates? Which changes need an audit trail? |  |
| Physical environment | If hardware is needed: what water exposure, handling, charging, battery duration, attachment, and visibility constraints apply? |  |
| Support and integration | Which existing tools or records must it work with? Who maintains it? What budget and volunteer capacity are available? |  |

### Validation and priority for each requirement

Record: rationale; consequence if deferred; priority; dependencies; validating owner; status; and unresolved questions. Suggested status: proposed, validated, deferred, or rejected. Mark assumptions explicitly.

Use Must for an essential pilot outcome or confirmed operating constraint; Should for a valuable need with a viable workaround; Could for an optional improvement; and Later for work excluded from the pilot. Confirm priorities against outcomes and delivery capacity.

## Worked example and interview close

Illustrative only. This example demonstrates the method and does not assert a CWB process or an agreed requirement.

### From evidence to a testable proposal

Evidence E-01 — Hypothetical manager report: “Yesterday we allocated a boat, then found out it needed maintenance. We had to change the visitor’s plan.”

Scenario SC-01 — Allocate a suitable boat when availability may have changed. User need: the person allocating the boat needs current serviceability information before confirming the allocation.

Use case UC-01 — Check eligibility and confirm a boat allocation. Actor: authorized staff member. Precondition: a boat record exists. Main flow: view status, select an eligible boat, validate eligibility, and confirm. Exception: eligibility changes before confirmation; the allocation is blocked and the reason is displayed.

Candidate FR-01 — The system shall prevent confirmation of a boat allocation when the boat is marked unavailable for maintenance. Trace: E-01 → SC-01 → UC-01 → FR-01.

Acceptance criterion — Given a boat marked unavailable for maintenance, when a staff member attempts to confirm its allocation, then no allocation is created and the maintenance restriction is displayed.

Candidate NFR-01 — A committed maintenance status change shall become visible to other active staff sessions within [TBD] seconds under [TBD peak conditions]. Verify using timed updates across concurrent sessions. Validate the target, relevant devices, and stale-data behavior with operational users.

Open questions — Who sets maintenance status? Who may restore serviceability? Are overrides permitted? What should happen if the status cannot be verified? What is the current frequency and impact of this problem?

Read back the three most important problems, their consequences, and your understanding of the desired outcomes. Ask the manager to correct them. Identify the staff, volunteers, and visitors whose perspectives still need to be heard; the manager’s account is one evidence source.
